# Palm Beach: Rules

*Working rules. Started 16 Nov 2026 from the printed **Rulebook PALM BEACH v4**; updated 17 Nov with the character card print files and on **19 Nov 2026** to match the InDesign rulebook v5 (20 pages: counters, Initiative, End of game, Game variations, FAQ) and later on 19 Nov with Lars's answers to the rules-editor review. **Solo mode rewritten 19 Nov 2026** after the solo review, approved by Lars, and laid out in the rulebook p16–18 the same day. Rule changes are Lars's decisions only.*

Changes from v4:
1. Bonus cards are no longer gold foil. **Bonus and landmark cards show their extra gold as a yellow circle (+1, +2 or +5) in the upper left corner.**
2. The **character powers** follow the character card print files (4 Nov 2026). Edith and Skip are now in Palm Beach; Leja and Silviano are Boca Raton only.
3. **Counters** (red Powers, yellow Re-rolls) and starting **Diamonds** are shown at the bottom of each character card.

1–5 players (solo mode against the Automa) · Ages 14+ · 45–60 minutes

## Objective

Welcome to Palm Beach. You play a famous face of the Gold Coast, past or present, competing for status. The player with the most **Prestige Points** at the end wins. *Eight rounds, ten auctions, one property: bid wisely.*

You earn Prestige Points in two ways:
- **Luxury Cards** (1–3 Prestige Points each), won in the game's **10 Luxury Auctions** or by trading in **Diamonds**.
- **The Property Card** (2–4 Prestige Points), won at the final Property Auction.

The currency is gold, written **Au**.

## Components used

- 2 Main Boards (Palm Beach side up)
- All 64 Map Cards: 8 Lagoon, 16 Waterfront (8 on the Lagoon side, 8 on the Atlantic side), 32 Block, 8 Atlantic
- 1 of the 3 Auction Map Cards
- 3 Property Cards: Mar-a-Lago (IV = 4 points), Gulf Stream Golf Course (III = 3), Spanish River Park (II = 2)
- 17 Luxury Cards: **5 × 3 points, 7 × 2 points, 5 × 1 point**. Three of the 1-point cards show re-rolls: one with 2, two with 1.
  - 3 points: submarine, rocket, hotel, sports team, superyacht
  - 2 points: private jet, gyrocopter, sports car, art gallery, racehorse, racing yacht, rings
  - 1 point: tiger (2 re-rolls), watch (1 re-roll), handbag (1 re-roll), shoes, wine
- 10 Character Cards (Palm Beach side)
- 16 acrylic Diamonds
- Wooden counters (10 × 10 mm): red = Powers, yellow = Re-rolls; one blue counter is the Automa marker in solo mode (blue Extra Turns are a Boca Raton rule)
- Gold bars: 15 × 10 Au, 15 × 5 Au, 30 × 1 Au
- 1 die

*No Player Aids.*

## Setup

1. **Boards and Map Cards.** Place the two Main Boards Palm Beach side up. Sort the 64 Map Cards by the type on their backs, shuffle each type, and lay them face down: rows 1–6 have 9 cards (Lagoon, Waterfront, 5 Block, Waterfront, Atlantic); rows 7 and 8 have 5 cards (Lagoon, Waterfront, Block, Waterfront, Atlantic), leaving room for the Auction Map Card.
2. **Auction Map Card.** Shuffle the 3 Auction Map Cards face down. Take 1 at random and return the other two to the box unseen. Place it at the end of rows 7 and 8.
3. **Luxury Cards.** Shuffle the 17 Luxury Cards. Place **10** face down to the right of the rows: one per row, and a **second card beside rows 4 and 6**. Place the remaining **7** face down on their space on the Main Board (the Diamond trade pile).
4. **Property Cards.** Put the 3 Property Cards in a pile at the end of row 8.
5. **Bank.** All gold bars and Diamonds go on the board as the Bank. **Each player starts with 5 Au**, unless their character card says otherwise.
6. **Characters.** Deal 2 Character Cards to each player; keep one (see Game variations). Take the counters and Diamonds shown at the bottom of your card. Kingston and Edith spend their red counter now.

## Gameplay

**8 rounds.** Round 1 is played on row 1, round 2 on row 2, and so on. Each round has three phases.

### 1. Starting the round (Initiative)
Everyone looks at their initiative number for the round (R1–R8) on their Character Card. **The lowest number starts the round and bids first in the auction.** No two characters share a number in any round, so there is never a tie.
*Example: Round 3. Edith has 80, Bright Shell 30 and Skip 60: Bright Shell starts.*

### 2. Card flipping phase
Starting with the start player and going clockwise, each player chooses a face-down card **in the current row** and tries to flip it.
- **Lagoon or Atlantic:** say which card, then roll. On a failed roll the card stays face down for a later attempt.
- **Block or Waterfront:** no roll; flip it and take the reward.

The phase ends when **all cards in the row** are face up (9 in rows 1–6, 5 in rows 7–8).

| Type | Die roll | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| **Lagoon**: high risk, big reward | Roll 4–6 | 8 Au |
| **Atlantic**: medium risk | Roll 3–6 | 6 Au |
| **Waterfront**: guaranteed diamond | None | 1 Diamond *(the card back shows +4 Au for Boca Raton and a Diamond for Palm Beach)* |
| **Block**: safe and steady | None | 3 Au |

**Bonus and landmark cards.** A yellow circle in the upper left corner shows extra gold, paid only when the card is flipped:

| Bonus | Cards | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **+5 Au** | 9 Block Bonus Cards | Block cards |
| **+2 Au** | 8 landmark cards | 1 Lagoon, 1 Waterfront (Lagoon side), 5 Block, 1 Atlantic *(from `js/landmarks.js`)* |
| **+1 Au** | 4 landmark cards | Waterfront (Atlantic side) |

**Automatic Diamond Trade.** The moment you have **4 Diamonds** (Mr. Flagler: 3), return them to the Bank and take the **top card of the face-down Luxury pile** on the Main Board (7 cards).

### 3. Auction phase: the Luxury Auction
After all cards in the row are flipped:
1. Turn the round's Luxury Card face up (1, 2 or 3 Prestige Points). In rounds 4 and 6 there are two cards: auction them one after the other. **The start player opens both auctions.**
2. The start player **bids first or passes**. Going clockwise, each player either **raises** or **passes**: the lowest bid is **1 Au** and each raise is at least 1 Au more. Once you pass, you're out of this auction. **You can never bid more gold than you have.** (No rule needed for "everyone passes".)
3. The last bidder pays the Bank and takes the card. Luxury Cards with re-rolls: take that many yellow counters (also when you get one from a Diamond trade).

Your gold is secret, so nobody knows exactly how much the others can bid.

### End of the round
Rounds 1–7: start a new round. Round 8: go to the Property Auction.

## End of game: the Property Auction

After the Luxury Auction in round 8, flip the Auction Map Card. It shows which Property Card is auctioned: return the other two to the box. **Characters who collect gold before that property's auction take it now** (Ms. Post 15 Au for Mar-a-Lago, Roxana 10 Au for Spanish River Park, Carina 10 Au for Gulf Stream Golf Course). Auction the Property Card with the same rules as a Luxury Auction (the start player may pass; lowest bid 1 Au). The winner pays the Bank and takes the card. Then the game ends.

## Final scoring & winning

Everyone adds up the Prestige Points on their Luxury Cards and Property Card.
- The player with the **most Prestige Points** wins.
- **Tiebreaker:** the tied player holding the Property Card wins. If no tied player has it, the tied player with the most remaining Au wins. If still tied, share the victory.

## Game variations

*Open: the card lies face up from the start. Closed: it stays face down until it is revealed.*

Luxury and Auction Map Cards:
- **Lake Worth (standard):** all Luxury Cards and the Auction Map Card face down.
- **Jupiter Inlet:** in rounds 4 and 6, the second Luxury Card lies face up from the start.
- **Peanut Island:** the Auction Map Card lies face up, so everyone knows which property is auctioned at the end. **Remove Kingston before choosing characters** (characters whose power is useless in a variation are removed).

Choosing characters:
- **Pick 2 (standard):** each player is dealt 2 characters and keeps 1.
- **Random:** each player is dealt 1 character. Good for beginners.
- **Open Draft:** lay all 10 face up. The player who rolls highest picks first, then clockwise. The initiative numbers still decide who starts each round.

## Counters and Diamonds

- **Red, Powers:** most Palm Beach powers work all game. If the card shows 0 red counters, no counter is needed. Kingston and Edith have 1 red counter: they spend it at the start of the game to secretly look at cards.
- **Yellow, Re-rolls:** whenever you roll for a Lagoon or Atlantic card, spend one and roll again; keep the new roll. Use as many as you have.
- **Diamonds:** count towards the Automatic Diamond Trade.
- A spent counter goes back to the box. New yellow counters only come from Luxury Cards with re-rolls (won at auction or from a Diamond trade).

## Solo mode: playing against the Automa (approved by Lars 19 Nov 2026; rulebook p16–18)

### Setup
Same as a normal game, except:
1. **Choose the difficulty. It decides the Automa's character:**
   - *Standard:* remove Edith, Kingston, Bright Shell and Eric. Shuffle the other 6 and draw one for the Automa.
   - *Hard:* the Automa plays **Bright Shell**.
   - *Expert:* the Automa plays **Eric**.
2. **Your character:** choose yours from the characters that are left, as in a normal game (Pick 2, Random or Open Draft).
3. **The Automa starts like a player:** 5 Au plus the counters and Diamonds shown on its card. Its gold lies open next to its card.
4. **Automa marker:** take one blue counter. It shows which card the Automa tried last.

### How the Automa plays
The Automa uses **all the powers on its character card**, its yellow re-rolls (on every failed roll) and the **Automatic Diamond Trade**, just like a player. The initiative numbers decide who flips first each round, as in the normal game.

### Automa: card flipping phase
On each turn, the Automa takes the first of these that it can:
1. **First turn of the round: Waterfront.** Rounds 1–6: the leftmost face-down Waterfront. Round 7: only if it has 1 or more Diamonds. Round 8: only if it has 2 or more.
2. **One Diamond short:** if it has 3 Diamonds (Mr. Flagler: 2), the leftmost face-down Waterfront.
3. **Otherwise:** the **next face-down card to the right of the marker**. Skip face-up cards; after the last card in the row, go on from the first card.

If there is no face-down Waterfront in step 1 or 2, go on to the next step. On a Lagoon or Atlantic card the Automa rolls as normal (and uses its re-rolls if a roll fails).

**The Automa marker (blue counter)**
- **Start of each round:** put the marker just to the left of the first card in the new row (the Lagoon).
- **After every Automa turn, put the marker on the card the Automa just tried**, always, whatever happened:
  - **The card was flipped** (any type, also a Waterfront from step 1 or 2): the marker goes on the face-up card.
  - **A Lagoon or Atlantic roll failed:** the marker goes on that card, which stays face down. On its next turn the Automa does not try it again straight away but moves on to the right. It comes back to the card only if it goes all the way round the row.
- **A face-down card with the marker on it is still a normal face-down card.** Every card in the row must be flipped before the round ends, so it will be attempted again: by you on one of your turns, or by the Automa when it comes round to it.
- **If you flip the card that has the marker on it, leave the marker where it is.** Only the Automa moves the marker. On its next turn it still counts from the marker.

*Example: round 2. The Automa's first turn is step 1, the Waterfront on the Lagoon side: it flips it, and the marker goes on that Waterfront. Its next turn is step 3: the next face-down card to the right is a Block, so it flips that and the marker moves onto the Block. Later it tries the Atlantic and rolls a 2: the Atlantic stays face down with the marker on it. On its next turn there are no more cards to the right, so it goes on from the first card in the row and tries the Lagoon, if it is still face down.*

### Automa: Luxury Auctions (one roll)
For each Luxury Card:
1. **You bid first.** Push the gold you want to bid forward, or hold it in your closed hand. You may bid 0. Decide before the Automa rolls.
2. **Roll for the Automa.** Its bid is the card's points × (8 + the roll); see the table. If it has less gold than that, it bids all its gold.
3. **The higher bid wins; a tie goes to the Automa.** The winner pays its bid to the Bank and takes the card.

| Automa's bid | Roll 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-point card | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 14 |
| 2-point card | 18 | 20 | 22 | 24 | 26 | 28 |
| 3-point card | 27 | 30 | 33 | 36 | 39 | 42 |

*Example: a 2-point card. You push 23 Au forward. You roll a 4 for the Automa, so it bids 24 Au. The Automa pays 24 Au and takes the card; you keep your 23 Au. Had you pushed 25 Au forward, you would have won and paid 25 Au.*

In rounds 4 and 6, auction the two cards one after the other, with a new roll for each.

### Automa: Property Auction
Collect any character gold for the Property first (also for the Automa). Then you bid as in a Luxury Auction. The Automa bids **all its gold**. **Hard:** roll the die and add **roll × 3 Au** to the Automa's bid. **Expert:** add **roll × 5 Au**. The higher bid wins; a tie goes to the Automa. If the Automa wins, it pays all its gold (the extra from the roll is only part of the bid).

### Winning
Same scoring and tiebreaker as the multiplayer game.

*Design note (not for the rulebook): simulated against a decent player, the Automa wins about 36 % at Standard, 50 % at Hard and 65 % at Expert. It makes about 21 die rolls per game, and the only look-up is the bid table.*

## FAQ

- **What information is public vs. private?** Only your gold (Au) is secret. Everything else is kept in view.
- **What is the main way to get Diamonds?** Flip Waterfront cards. Some characters also start with Diamonds.
- **Do I get the bonus on a failed roll?** No. You only get the bonus gold when the card is flipped.
- **Can I bid more gold than I have?** No, never.
- **Who opens the auctions in rounds 4 and 6?** The player who started the round opens both auctions, one after the other.
- **When does Ms. Post get her 15 Au?** When the Auction Map Card shows Mar-a-Lago, before that auction starts. Roxana and Carina collect their 10 Au the same way.
- **When do you flip the Auction Map Card?** After the Luxury Auction in round 8. The Property Auction is the last thing in the game.

**FAQ solo mode**
- **Is the Automa's gold secret?** No. It lies open next to its card.
- **Does the Automa use its character's power?** Yes, plus its re-rolls and the Automatic Diamond Trade.
- **Who flips first?** The initiative numbers, as in the normal game.
- **What if the Automa fails a roll on a Lagoon or Atlantic card?** The card stays face down with the marker on it. Next turn, the Automa moves on to the right.
- **Where does the marker go after a successful flip?** On the card the Automa just flipped, also a Waterfront from its first turn.
- **What if I flip the card that has the marker on it?** Leave the marker there. Only the Automa moves it.
- **Does it matter if I show my bid?** No. The Automa's bid comes only from the die. Just decide before you roll.
- **What if I bid 0?** The Automa takes the card for its bid (for 0 if it has no gold: a tie goes to the Automa).
