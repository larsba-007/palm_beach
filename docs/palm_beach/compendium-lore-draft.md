## The three properties (Compendium p9)

### Mar-a-Lago (IV)
Cereal heiress Marjorie Merriweather Post searched for years for a site between the ocean and the lake, which is what the name means. Built between 1924 and 1927 at the height of the Florida land boom, the estate was designed by architect Marion Sims Wyeth, with interiors and ornament by the Viennese stage designer Joseph Urban. Today it is a National Historic Landmark.

### Gulf Stream Golf Course (III)
Founded in 1924 in the small seaside town of Gulf Stream, the club has a course laid out by the legendary golf architect Donald Ross and a Mediterranean Revival clubhouse by Addison Mizner. It is private and by invitation only, which makes winning it at auction all the sweeter.

### Spanish River Park (II)
A 95-acre park on Boca Raton's barrier island, stretching from the Intracoastal Waterway to the Atlantic. Tunnels under the coastal road lead to the beach, and a 40-foot observation tower looks out over both waters. The park is named after a stream, once called Boca Raton's Lagoon, whose old bed can still be traced. In the 1960s and 70s, Boca Raton voters approved bonds to keep this stretch of coast open to the public.

## Who's who: the 12 characters (Compendium p10)

*From the coast's past*

**Bright Shell, Jaega Tribe.** Long before the mansions, the Jaega people lived on these barrier islands and along the lagoon, living from the sea. Bright Shell walks the shoreline looking for coquina and moon snail shells. She doesn't need powers: she knows every wave, and she always gets another try.

**Mr. Flagler, the Industrialist.** Henry Flagler, co-founder of Standard Oil, brought his Florida East Coast Railway to the shores of Lake Worth in 1894 and opened the Royal Poinciana Hotel the same year, followed by The Breakers. Palm Beach became America's winter playground. He is always first off the train.

**Addison, the Architect.** In 1925, Palm Beach society architect Addison Mizner set out to turn Boca Raton into "the world's most architecturally beautiful playground". His Cloister Inn opened in 1926, just as the land boom collapsed. His Mediterranean style still shapes the coast, and he never misses a full row.

**Ms. Post, the Heiress.** Inspired by Marjorie Merriweather Post, heiress to her father's cereal business and builder of Mar-a-Lago. She collected art, gave grand parties and knew the value of everything. Whatever you want to buy, she has made it a little more expensive.

*From the coast today*

**Roxana, the Actress.** A screen star who spends the winter season on the coast, far from the studios. She loves the beach at Spanish River Park and an audience even more. If she needs the spotlight, she simply makes you wait a turn.

**Eric, the Tech Mogul.** Boca Raton has a tech history: the old IBM campus, today the Boca Raton Innovation Campus. Eric made his fortune in software and now buys waterfront by the handful, three lots at a time.

**Leja, the Pop Artist.** Leja paints the Gold Coast in bright colours and sells to the collectors of Palm Beach. She knows that what's on top of the pile isn't always what matters: sometimes the best piece is underneath.

**Silviano, the Fashion Designer.** Silviano's boutiques line the most elegant shopping streets on the coast. He never shops just once: one deal in the morning, another in the afternoon.

**Kingston, the Tennis Player.** A former champion who now plays the club courts by the ocean. He spends every morning on the waterfront and always comes home with a little extra.

**Carina, the Golfer.** A tour professional who knows every green between Palm Beach and Boca Raton. She marks her favourite spots so no one else gets there easily, and she is always the one invited to the Gulf Stream auction.

**Edith, the Flamingo Queen.** Hostess of the most talked-about garden parties on the island, always in pink. She knows what's coming before anyone else, because she has already peeked at the guest list.

**Skip, Reef Road Waterman.** Skip surfs Reef Road in Palm Beach, known since the 1960s as Florida's big-wave spot. Where everyone else hesitates at the Atlantic, he just paddles out.
